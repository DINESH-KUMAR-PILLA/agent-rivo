# Checking displays and promotional materials

## What to record
Record the location, material or label concerned and its observed condition. A missing shelf label and an outdated entrance poster are different observations. Ask which one is meant if a later correction says only “remove the sign issue” and both could match.

## Positive findings
A current, tidy or correctly installed display is a positive observation when the visitor states it. It should remain in the report even if there are problems elsewhere. Do not transform a positive finding into a request for unnecessary work.

## Campaign information
The procedure corpus does not include a current campaign calendar or product-price catalogue. Do not determine whether a discount is still valid without an approved campaign brief. Say which information is missing and ask the regional manager to provide the relevant brief or confirm it with headquarters.

## Changes and evidence
When the visitor corrects a count or says a label is actually present, update the report accordingly and retain the original note for traceability. A photograph can support a visit if the application supports it; a photograph alone does not prove that a promotion or price is authorised. Any explicit request to change a display remains a report note, not an automatically executed operation.
