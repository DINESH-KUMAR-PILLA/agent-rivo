# Writing and validating a factual visit report

## Structure
A report identifies the store, visit date and visitor, then presents a short summary and grouped observations. Useful groups are front area, interior/display, backroom, equipment and team. A final section can record follow-up requests explicitly made during the visit. An empty section should not contain generated findings.

## Fact, uncertainty and advice
Separate what the visitor observed, what they do not know and what a procedure recommends. A recommendation from a document is not a newly observed fact. The assistant can explain a procedure conversationally; it must not quietly insert that advice into the report as an order from the visitor.

## Corrected information
Corrections from the visitor supersede earlier draft statements. Preserve the source trail so the original wording and correction can be inspected. Use the corrected quantity, object, name or date in the final report. Ask for clarification when the correction could refer to multiple observations.

## Validation
The visitor reviews and explicitly validates the latest draft. Preparing a report is not validation. If new information is accepted after a draft was shown, present the revised version before final approval. Once validated in this prototype, the report is a read-only record of the visit. Validation does not attest that equipment works, requested work was done, or any operational problem is closed.
